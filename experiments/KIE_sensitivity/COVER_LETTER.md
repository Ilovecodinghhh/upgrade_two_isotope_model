# Cover Letter

**To the Editors of Global Biogeochemical Cycles / Atmospheric Chemistry and Physics**

Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled **"Reducing Kinetic Isotope Effect Sensitivity in Methane Source Apportionment via a Dual-Isotope Agreement Filter"** for consideration as a research article.

## Significance

Resolving whether rising atmospheric methane is driven primarily by fossil fuel or microbial sources is one of the most consequential questions in contemporary climate science — with direct implications for emission reduction policy. Current isotope-enabled models attribute the post-2006 CH₄ rise largely to microbial emissions based on declining atmospheric δ¹³C-CH₄, but this attribution depends critically on the kinetic isotope effect (KIE) for the CH₄ + OH reaction, where two laboratory measurements (Saueressig et al., 2001: 1.0039; Cantrell et al., 1990: 1.0054) have remained unreconciled for over three decades. The 0.15% difference between them propagates to a 20–40 Tg yr⁻¹ uncertainty in source partitioning — comparable to the entire post-2006 trend.

## Novel Contributions

Our manuscript makes three contributions that we believe are new to the literature:

1. **Negative result with broad implications:** We demonstrate rigorously that coupling δD into joint optimizations (WLS, variational) with δ¹³C *amplifies* rather than reduces KIE sensitivity — a counterintuitive finding with immediate practical relevance for modeling groups developing dual-isotope inversion frameworks.

2. **A new diagnostic method:** The "Agreement Filter" — solving δ¹³C and δD independently and filtering by inter-isotope consistency — reduces KIE sensitivity by a factor of 2.5–3.2×. We define the KIE Sensitivity Ratio (KSR) as a standardized metric for comparing approaches.

3. **A novel observational KIE discriminant:** We discover that the δ¹³C–δD agreement rate itself distinguishes between the two competing KIE values. Cantrell's value yields 68% inter-isotope agreement vs. 44% for Saueressig (p < 0.05). This provides observational evidence favoring the higher KIE independent of assumptions about source-signature values — a fundamentally different approach from prior arguments.

## Relevance to Community Practice

Our survey of 16 recent studies shows that Saueressig's lower KIE (1.0039) remains the most commonly adopted default value. Combined with recent findings by He et al. (2026, JGR) showing that this value produces unphysical emission estimates, and new laboratory measurements by Whitehill et al. (2023) supporting values ≥1.0054, our work contributes to a growing body of evidence suggesting the need for a course correction in community practice.

## Scope

This work is directly relevant to the scope of [GBC/ACP] and should be of broad interest to the atmospheric methane community, isotope geochemists, and Earth system modelers. The method is computationally lightweight (box-model level) and immediately applicable as a diagnostic for existing 3D inverse modeling systems.

We confirm that this work is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not under consideration by any other journal.
