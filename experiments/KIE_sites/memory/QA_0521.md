## Q3: How did you assign wetland emissions to each site?
**A:** Sites are background air monitors, not local emitters. Aggregated Li2026 1°×1° emissions into 4 latitude bands (NH high 60–90°N, NH mid 30–60°N, Tropics 30°S–30°N, SH extra 90–30°S). Assigned by air-mass origin:
- NH sites → their latitude band (ALT/ZEP/BRW → NH_high; CBA/MHD → NH_mid; KUM → Tropics)
- SH sites → SH_extra (not Global), because NH seasonal signal is attenuated ~88% by interhemispheric transport (τ_mix ≈ 1.3 yr)

**Critical fix:** Initial Global assignment for CGO/SPO was wrong — overcorrected by 7×. SH_extra gives physically correct small corrections (14–18% of observed signal).

## Q4: How did you deal with the sink–source phase shift?
**A:** OH peaks ~June, wetlands peak ~July–August (1–2 month offset). Scalar subtraction would be wrong. Solution: **phasor decomposition** — treat harmonic coefficients as complex numbers Z = B + iC, then vector-subtract:

```
Z_sink = Z_obs − Z_source
```

This automatically handles any phase offset. Key subtlety: the source–atmosphere isotope gap is **negative** (δ_wetland < δ_atm for both isotopes), which flips the source phasor 180° — wetlands peak in July but their isotopic *effect* (pulling δ down) points toward January in the phasor plane. The observed small amplitude at NH sites is because source and sink vectors **nearly cancel** (anti-aligned). After subtraction, the sink amplitude is much larger than observed.

**Consistency check:** After subtraction, sink phases for δ¹³C and δD agree within <1 month at all sites — confirms a common OH-driven signal was isolated.
