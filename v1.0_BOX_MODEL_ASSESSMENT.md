# The Most Original Two-Isotope Box Model: Its Scientific Assessment & Improvement Roadmap

## 1. What This Model Does

The `TwoIsotopeBoxModel` is a **one-box, dual-isotope (δ¹³C + δD) Monte Carlo mass-balance model** that partitions global methane emissions into three source categories:
- **Biomass burning (BB)**
- **Fossil fuel (FF)**
- **Microbial (Mic)**

For each year (1999–2022), the model:
1. Calculates total global CH₄ sources from observed [CH₄] changes + assumed lifetime (9 yr)
2. Uses observed atmospheric δ¹³C-CH₄ and δD-CH₄ to derive the isotopic composition of the aggregate source
3. Solves a 3×3 linear system (mass + δ¹³C + δD) to partition emissions among three end-members
4. Propagates uncertainty through 1,000 Monte Carlo iterations sampling source signatures from pre-computed distributions

Sensitivity tests explore: reduced Cl sink, increased OH sink, and reduced biomass burning.

## 2. Strengths & Scientific Potential

### 2.1 The Dual-Isotope Advantage

**This is the model's most significant contribution.** The vast majority of published isotopic budget studies use δ¹³C alone (Nisbet 2016, Schaefer 2016, Worden 2017, Basu 2022, Chandra 2024, Thanwerdas 2024). A single isotope constrains only two unknowns (e.g., fossil vs. microbial), requiring biomass burning to be externally prescribed. Your model uses **both δ¹³C and δD simultaneously**, which uniquely allows all three major source categories to be solved from isotopic constraints alone — without fixing any source a priori.

This is a genuine scientific advance. Only a handful of studies have attempted dual-isotope budgets (Monteil et al. 2011; Warwick et al. 2016 for the Arctic), and none with the Monte Carlo uncertainty propagation framework you've built.

### 2.2 Monte Carlo Uncertainty Framework

The 1,000-iteration MC sampling across source signatures, atmospheric observations (DEI iterations), and inventory weightings (EDGAR vs CT-CH₄) provides a robust uncertainty envelope. This is methodologically sound and superior to the single-point-estimate approach used in many published studies.

### 2.3 Time-Varying Source Signatures

Using annually varying source signatures from Tapin/Thanwerdas (2026) and your own compilations — rather than static global means — is critical. Feinberg et al. (2018) showed that spatially and temporally varying signatures can shift inversion results by several Tg/yr. Your approach is consistent with current best practice.

### 2.4 Sensitivity Test Architecture

The four scenarios (base, reduced Cl, increased OH, reduced BB) address the key axes of uncertainty in the methane budget debate. This structured approach to sensitivity analysis is exactly what the field needs.

## 3. Critical Limitations & Scientific Concerns

### 3.1 The Naus (2019) Critique: Transport Neglect

**This is the most fundamental limitation.** Naus et al. (2019, ACP) demonstrated that one-box models systematically misattribute inter-hemispheric transport signals as source changes. The one-box framework assumes instantaneous global mixing, which:

- Aliases NH/SH emission asymmetries into apparent global source changes
- Cannot distinguish between a tropical wetland increase and a boreal wetland increase (different δ¹³C!)
- Introduces ~10–15 Tg/yr biases in inferred source changes on interannual timescales

**Impact on your model:** The 3×3 linear system is solved at global scale, but real sources have strong latitudinal gradients. A tropical microbial increase and a boreal fossil fuel decrease could produce similar global-mean δ¹³C changes but would be resolved differently in a 2-box or 3D model.

**Severity: HIGH** — this is a structural limitation inherent to the one-box approach.

### 3.2 No CH₄-OH Chemical Feedback

**Nguyen et al. (2020) showed this matters.** Your model uses a fixed 9-year lifetime with static sink fractions. But:

- The CH₄-CO-OH system has a perturbation lifetime of ~13.2 years, not 9.4 years
- A 20 Tg/yr emission increase depletes OH, which your model doesn't capture
- Ignoring this feedback leads to a 25% overestimation of emission changes after 10 years
- Declining CO concentrations since the 2000s have increased OH availability, reducing the effective CH₄ lifetime — your model treats lifetime as constant

**Prather (2024, JAMES)** further showed that the nonlinear CH₄-OH feedback becomes increasingly important as CH₄ concentrations rise — precisely the regime since 2007.

**He et al. (2026, Science)** found that the effective CH₄ lifetime has not been constant: it changed measurably over 1980–2020, with implications for all box-model inversions that assume fixed lifetime.

**Severity: HIGH** — especially for interpreting the post-2007 acceleration.

### 3.3 The KIE_OH Uncertainty

The model uses Cantrell (1990) OH KIE = 1.0054 for ¹³C, with Saueressig (2001) = 1.0039 as an alternative. **This single parameter choice shifts the entire budget by ~20–30 Tg/yr** between fossil and microbial categories (as noted by Chandra et al. 2024, who showed 1.2‰ differences in simulated δ¹³C between Saueressig and Cantrell values).

For δD, the situation is even worse: OH KIE_D = 1.294 (Saueressig) vs. ~1.327 (Whitehill-Joelson average), a 2.5% difference that propagates strongly through the δD mass balance.

The model currently runs with fixed KIE values per scenario. **A KIE uncertainty envelope is essential** — sampling KIE_OH from a distribution (e.g., uniform [1.0039, 1.0054] for ¹³C) within the MC loop would better characterize this dominant uncertainty.

**Severity: HIGH** — potentially the largest single source of systematic error.

### 3.4 The δD Data Quality Issue

The atmospheric δD-CH₄ dataset is significantly less mature than δ¹³C:
- The global δD DEI (deseasonalized envelope integrated) record has fewer stations, larger measurement uncertainties, and potential intercalibration issues
- The `GlobMean_dD_iterations_UmezawaCal_noBUDS.xlsx` data starts later (2005) and is padded backward to 1999 by repeating the first value — introducing artificial stability
- Source signature δD uncertainties are much larger than δ¹³C (as your code shows: `mic_dd_U = 7` is hardcoded, not from the data)

**Severity: MEDIUM** — the δD constraint is the model's unique advantage, but it's also its weakest observational link.

### 3.5 Linear System Ill-Conditioning

The 3×3 system can become ill-conditioned when source end-members are close in isotopic space. For δ¹³C, fossil fuel (−44‰) and microbial (−62‰) are well-separated, but for δD, the separation is smaller and more variable. When the matrix A has a low condition number, small errors in the atmospheric observations or source signatures produce large swings in the solution — potentially yielding negative emissions for individual categories.

The code handles `LinAlgError` but doesn't explicitly check for or report the frequency of non-physical solutions (negative emissions). This is worth monitoring.

**Severity: MEDIUM** — may explain some of the MC spaghetti scatter.

### 3.6 Missing Minor Sources

The three-category partition (BB, FF, Mic) lumps together sub-categories with very different signatures:
- **Microbial** includes wetlands (−62‰ δ¹³C), rice paddies (−63‰), ruminants (−66‰), and waste (−55‰) — spanning a 10‰ range
- **Fossil fuel** includes thermogenic gas (−40‰), coal (−35‰), and oil (−45‰) — also a wide range
- Geological seeps, freshwater emissions, and termites are either misassigned or ignored

**Severity: LOW to MEDIUM** — inherent to any 3-source partition; your MC sampling of within-category variability partially addresses this.

## 4. Specific Improvement Recommendations

### 4.1 Upgrade to a Two-Hemisphere Box Model (Priority: ★★★★★)

This is the single most impactful improvement. Following Nguyen et al. (2020):

- Split into Northern and Southern Hemisphere boxes with 1-year interhemispheric exchange time
- Allow separate NH/SH source strengths and signatures
- This eliminates the Naus (2019) transport critique and enables testing of whether the rise is driven by NH (industrial) or SH/tropical (wetland) changes
- **Implementation cost: moderate** — the mathematical framework is well-established in Nguyen (2020) and Prather (1994)

### 4.2 Add Interactive CH₄-CO-OH Chemistry (Priority: ★★★★☆)

Following Nguyen et al. (2020) and Holmes (2018):

- Add CO as a state variable (data from NOAA)
- Make OH reactive: OH = S_OH / (k₁[CH₄] + k₂[CO] + k₃[X])
- This automatically gives the correct ~13.2-year perturbation lifetime
- Allows CO concentration trends (declining since 2000) to affect the inversion
- **Implementation cost: moderate** — Nguyen (2020) provides the full equation set (their Table A1)

### 4.3 Sample KIE Within the Monte Carlo Loop (Priority: ★★★★☆)

Currently, KIE is fixed per scenario. Instead:
- For each MC iteration, draw KIE_OH(¹³C) from Uniform(1.0039, 1.0054) or a literature-derived distribution
- Draw KIE_OH(D) from Uniform(1.294, 1.327)
- Draw KIE_Cl from its uncertainty range
- This properly propagates the dominant systematic uncertainty into the solution envelope
- **Implementation cost: trivial** — just add 4 random draws to the MC loop

### 4.4 Add Physical Constraints on Solutions (Priority: ★★★☆☆)

- Reject MC iterations where any source < 0 or > total, and report the rejection rate
- Alternatively, use a Bayesian framework with prior distributions on each source (e.g., FF ~N(100, 30), Mic ~N(370, 50), BB ~N(30, 10)) rather than solving unconstrained
- Consider switching from direct matrix inversion to a constrained least-squares or MCMC approach that respects positivity
- **Implementation cost: low to moderate**

### 4.5 Extend the δD Record (Priority: ★★★☆☆)

- Incorporate the Douglas et al. (2021) freshwater δD compilation for better microbial δD priors
- Use Brussee et al. (2026) triple-isotope data for geological source discrimination
- Consider incorporating Fujita et al. (2025) and Chen et al. (2025) source signature data for regional sensitivity tests
- **Implementation cost: low** — mainly data integration

### 4.6 Add ¹⁴C as Optional Third Isotope Constraint (Priority: ★★★☆☆)

- Hmiel et al. (2020) and the growing ¹⁴C-CH₄ record provide a powerful fossil/modern discriminant
- A 3-isotope (¹³C + D + ¹⁴C), 4-source model could partition fossil fuel, geological, microbial, and pyrogenic sources
- This requires ¹⁴C atmospheric time series (limited but growing) and source signatures (Brussee 2026, González-Moguel 2022)
- **Implementation cost: moderate to high** — requires ¹⁴C data compilation

### 4.7 Temporally Dynamic Lifetime (Priority: ★★★☆☆)

- Rather than fixed τ = 9 yr, use He et al. (2026) or Prather (2024) estimates of time-varying lifetime
- Or: Zhu et al. (2026) showed that multi-tracer approaches (MCF + ¹⁴CO) can constrain OH trends; integrate those constraints
- **Implementation cost: low** — just replace `Lifetime = 9` with a year-dependent array

### 4.8 Code Quality Improvements (Priority: ★★☆☆☆)

- Factor the shared logic across the 4 scenarios into a function (currently copy-pasted 4 times)
- Replace the hardcoded `mic_dd_U = 7` with data-driven uncertainty
- Add condition number monitoring for the A matrix
- Add a summary of non-physical solutions (% of iterations with negative sources)
- The backward-padding of δD data (repeating 2005 values for 1999–2004) should be documented or handled more carefully

**Your unique niche:** The only model that simultaneously uses δ¹³C + δD to partition all three source categories in a Monte Carlo framework. The 3D models (Basu, Thanwerdas, Chandra) have better spatial resolution and transport but only use δ¹³C. Nguyen (2020) has interactive chemistry but doesn't use isotopes for source partitioning.

**Publication potential:** A 2-hemisphere version with interactive OH chemistry, KIE uncertainty sampling, and the dual-isotope constraint would be a genuinely novel contribution — there is no published model that combines all of these.

## 7. Bottom Line

This model has **high scientific potential** precisely because of its dual-isotope approach — it addresses the exact capability gap identified in the literature synthesis: δD-CH₄ is severely underutilized in budget studies. The main risks are the structural limitations of the one-box framework and the large KIE uncertainties, both of which are addressable with the improvements outlined above.

With the two-hemisphere upgrade and interactive chemistry, this model would fill a genuine niche in the published literature: **the first dual-isotope, interactive-chemistry hemispheric box model for methane source partitioning.** That's a publishable contribution.
